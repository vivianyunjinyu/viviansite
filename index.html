a real-time, environment-aware spatializer

  • Debugged and optimized HRTF interpolation algorithms for spatial audio using SOFA-format datasets.
  • Built preprocessing that auto-orients SOFA files and maps their measurements onto a consistent coordinate system, then applies HRIR truncation and normalization.
  • Developed a generic KNN triangle-mesh builder so interpolation works across any SOFA file, not just one dataset.

in plain english: an HRTF is the fingerprint your head, ears and shoulders leave on a sound before it reaches your eardrum. Measure enough of them and you can place a sound anywhere around a listener wearing ordinary headphones.

up next

adaptive soundbeaming

ongoing

Soundbeaming steers a narrow column of sound out of a speaker array so it reaches one listener instead of filling the room. The adaptive part is the beam re-aiming itself as that listener moves.

glossary corner

HRTF
head-related transfer function: how a body colors incoming sound
SOFA
Spatially Oriented Format for Acoustics
KNN
k-nearest neighbors: the handful of measured directions closest to the one you actually need

Theramen contactless synthesizer

C++ · Daisy Seed (STM32H750) · ongoing

Two time-of-flight sensors read the distance to each hand and map it to continuous pitch and volume in a theremin-style. Both ship with the same I2C address, so each one is reassigned at startup. One-pole filtering smooths the glide, and the audio callback stays decoupled from sensor polling.

  • embedded
  • DSP
  • I²C
  • real-time

Vocoduh FFT channel vocoder

C++ · JUCE · may 2026

A real-time channel vocoder with overlap-add STFT (Kaiser windowing), mel-scale band grouping, and per-band envelope smoothing. Carrier whitening and weighted noise injection keep unvoiced consonants intelligible, with sidechain routing and a custom animated GUI.

  • STFT/IFFT
  • mel-scale
  • plugin
  • UI

Whoosh tempo-synced LFO

C++ · JUCE · june 2026

A host-synced LFO for rhythmic volume shaping and sidechain-style ducking. The modulation curve is drawn right in the UI with a draggable two-breakpoint editor, backed by a 10-bit lookup table that regenerates on a dirty flag instead of on every block.
